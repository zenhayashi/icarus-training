SEC 0101

What success looks like

By week 12 after approval.

  • Every new Member has done the intro and the Medical class within their first 30 days.
  • Every guild has two trained instructors, so no class depends on one person.
  • At least three guilds run a basic class on a repeating schedule.
  • Guild leaders are people who have already taught, not only people who were keen.

Design rules Recommended

Short and hands-on. Every lesson has one named owner. Two people can teach any class. Everything lives in documents the org owns, with Discord used only for sign-up and reminders. Build one guild first and copy what worked.

SEC 0303

How a class is run

Basic class: 45 to 60 minutes, 4 to 8 people per instructor.

T+0minutesT+60
  • Brief. What the guild does for the org, what it gets wrong most often, and the one safety or money mistake to avoid.
  • Demo. The instructor does the core task once, out loud.
  • Hands-on. Everyone does it while the instructor walks around. Nobody only watches.
  • Check-off. Each person does the practical task alone. Not passing is not failing: the instructor notes what to fix and the person joins the next session.
  • What's next. Guild channels, how to take or file a work order, when the next and the advanced class run.

Advanced class · about 90 minutes

A realistic scenario using the guild's work orders (30 min), deeper skills (30 min), and how to teach (30 min): running the five steps, giving feedback, handing out and following up work.

One page per lesson

Guild and level · owner · second reviewer · patch last checked · one-sentence outcome · what to bring · brief · demo script · hands-on task · check-off line · three common mistakes · links. A lesson cannot run until the owner has filled it and the reviewer has read it.

Instructor checklist · per class

Lesson current for this patch · room and time posted · sign-up list seen · each check-off ticked or noted · two-question feedback asked · record sent to the leader within 24 hours.

SEC 0606

Who teaches, and how leaders are chosen

  • Getting instructors. Each guild leader names two volunteers who are strong in that role. Teaching is invited, never assigned.
  • Training the trainer. The how-to-teach section of the advanced class, then one co-taught and one observed class.
  • Leader job. Run the guild's classes and keep lessons current; assign and approve the guild's work orders (leader roles and hidden channels already exist and work for this); welcome new members; send the admins a short monthly note of who finished what.
  • Succession. A leader trains their own replacement. After 30 quiet days the admins ask the Deputy to step in. A leader is not removed without a conversation.
  • Recognition. Teaching and attending are logged as contributions on the appreciation board (admin-only, already built).

SEC 0808

Sign-up, scheduling, records

  • Cadence. Each guild runs a basic class monthly, more often when five or more people are waiting. Orientation runs monthly, or when three or more new Members are waiting.
  • Sign-up. The instructor posts the class with the meeting command. People press Going, Maybe or Can't make it and get reminders an hour before and at the start. The guild's voice channel is the room. This already works without the AI.
  • Records. One shared sheet or pinned list per guild: name, class, date, instructor, pass or retry, patch. The leader keeps it and posts a monthly summary to the admins. Automating it later is optional.
  • Materials. A pinned post in the guild channel plus a copy in the org's shared documents, so any instructor can run the class.
  • Nudges. A Member who passes 14 days without the Medical class gets a friendly reminder from a person, not from the AI.
